1.1 · Introduction to Communication

What is communication?

DefinitionCommunication is the process of sharing messages from one point to another. It is the transfer of information from one or more people to other people.

  • The word "communication" comes from the Latin word "Communicare", which means to share.
  • Humankind has communicated in many ways since the beginning, by means of drawings, writings, signals, symbols and languages.
  • A conversation between two persons is an example of communication.
1.1 · Introduction to Communication

Purposeful exchange

Communication is a purposeful activity of exchanging ideas, data and information among people using various technical or natural means.

  • It is a two-way process of reaching mutual understanding.
  • Participants not only exchange information, news, ideas and feelings, but also create and share meaning.
  • It is a means of connecting people.

Symbolic or verbal means that people and machines use to make contact and share information

NewspapersMailEmailTelephonesTelevisionRadio
1.1 · Telecommunication

Telecommunication

DefinitionTelecommunication is the transmission of messages over significant distances for the purpose of communication.

Some examples of telecommunication

Mobile phonesLandlinesSatellite phonesVoIPRadioTelevisionNetworks
  • It enables people to communicate over long distances. When you phone a friend, you are using a form of telecommunication.
  • It can exist on open networks, where anyone can have access, or closed networks, where only certain people can send and receive data.
1.1 · Telecommunication

A telecommunication system

A complete telecommunication system has two stations. Each station has a transmitter to send signals and a receiver to receive signals.

  • Networks are made up of a collection of transmitters (devices used to send the message) and receivers (devices used to receive the message).
  • Signals can travel through wires or cables, optical fiber, or through the air using electromagnetic waves.
1.1 · Telecommunication

Key terms

Transceiver

Sometimes the transmitter and receiver are combined into one device called a transceiver.

Wireless communication

When data is sent through the air without wires, it is called wireless communication.

Telecommunication network

Many stations send and receive data together. The Internet is the largest example of a telecommunication network.

1.1 · Data Communication

Data communication

DefinitionData communication refers to the sharing of data in various forms like text, images, sound, etc. It is the transmission of data or information between a source and a receiver.

  • The source transmits the data and the receiver receives it.
  • Emails, instant messages and digital phone calls are examples of data communication.
  • Data communication is the transmission of electronic data and information between two points, either via an analogue (electrical) signal or a digital (binary) signal.

1.1 · Data Communication

Data / Message and Sender

1. Data / Message

Data refers to raw facts, figures, symbols or information that is to be transmitted. It is the basic content that a sender wants to share with a receiver. Data can exist as text, numbers, images, audio or video.

2. Sender

The person, computer or any device used for sending data is called the sender, source or transmitter. In a modern digital communication system, the source is usually a computer.

1.1 · Data Communication

Medium and Receiver

3. Medium

The means through which data flows from one location to another is called the transmission medium. Wired or wireless mediums are used to connect the receiver and the sender.

4. Receiver

The computer or any device that receives the data signal and converts it to its original form is called the receiver. It can be a computer, a printer or a fax machine.

1.1 · Data Communication

Protocols

5. ProtocolsThe set of rules and formats under which data transmission takes place between sender and receiver is called protocols.

  • The data communication software is used to transfer data from one computer to another.
  • Software that follows the same communication protocols can communicate and exchange data.
  • Example: in a conversation between persons A and B, A is the sender, air or cable is the medium and B is the receiver. The purpose is to get the message across clearly and unambiguously. This takes effort from both the sender and the receiver.
1.1 · Modes of Data Transmission

Modes of data transmission

The way in which data is transmitted from one location to another, or from one point to another, through different communication channels is called the data transmission mode.

Simplex mode

One direction only.

Duplex mode

Both directions.

Duplex mode has two types: half duplex and full duplex.

1.1 · Modes of Data Transmission

Simplex mode

  • Data is transmitted in only one direction. A terminal can only send data or only receive it, but cannot do both.
  • The receiver is passive and does not respond to the sender. There is no return signal, so it is not possible to confirm successful transmission.
  • Examples: Radio and Television broadcasts. It is the cheapest communication method.
1.1 · Modes of Data Transmission

Duplex mode

Duplex modeThe communication mode in which communication takes place in both directions.

  • The receiver is not passive. It can respond to the sender.
  • A device can be a sender and a receiver of the data signal at the same time.
  • Examples: telephone, e-mail, walkie-talkie.

Half duplex

Both directions, one at a time.

Full duplex

Both directions at the same time.

1.1 · Modes of Data Transmission

Half duplex mode

  • Data can be transmitted in both directions, but only in one direction at a time. One is the transmitter and the other is the receiver.
  • Each time the direction is reversed, which slows down the data transmission rate. Transmission of data can be confirmed.
  • Examples: walkie-talkie and fax machine. Each person takes turns talking. If both talk at once, nothing occurs.
1.1 · Modes of Data Transmission

Full duplex mode

  • Data can be transmitted in both directions simultaneously.
  • It is faster, because no time is wasted in switching directions.
  • Example: a telephone set, where both users can talk and listen at the same time. In computer networking, modems are configured to flow data in both directions.

1.1 · Broadband

Broadband

DefinitionBroadband is a high-speed internet connection that enables you to instantly send and receive data such as videos, music and websites.

Unlike traditional dial-up connections, broadband is always on and does not interfere with your phone line. It allows uninterrupted video streaming and online gaming.

Fast internet connection

Always connected

No need to dial.

Used for

Streaming, downloading, browsing and video calls.

1.1 · Types of Broadband

Types of broadband (1 of 2)

DSL (Digital Subscriber Line)

Internet through telephone lines. Users can use the phone and internet at the same time. Used in Nepal by Nepal Telecom before fiber became common.

ADSL (Asymmetric DSL)

A type of DSL where download speed is faster than upload. Suitable for home users who download more than they upload.

Cable Broadband

Internet via TV cables (coaxial). Faster than DSL, but speed may reduce if many users share it.

Satellite Broadband

Internet via satellites. Useful in remote or mountainous areas, such as some Himalayan regions of Nepal.

Fixed Wireless Broadband

Uses radio signals to provide internet to a fixed location. No cables needed. Used in rural and semi-urban areas of Nepal.

1.1 · Types of Broadband

Types of broadband (2 of 2)

WiMAX

Wireless internet covering towns or cities using microwave signals. Replaced mostly by fiber and 4G in Nepal.

Mobile Broadband (3G, 4G, 5G)

Internet on smartphones or portable devices through mobile networks. 4G is widely available in Nepal; 5G testing has begun.

Wi-Fi Broadband

Wireless internet within a limited area like home or school, using a router connected to fiber or DSL.

Fiber-Optic Broadband

Internet through fiber cables using light signals. Very fast, reliable and modern. Provided by WorldLink, Vianet and Nepal Telecom.

1.1 · Bandwidth and Throughput

Bandwidth

DefinitionBandwidth is the amount of data that can be transmitted through a communication channel during a specified period of time. It is the data handling capacity of a communication system.

  • The bandwidth of a digital signal is measured in bits per second (bps). The bandwidth of an analog signal is measured in cycles per second (cycle/sec) or Hertz.
  • The bandwidth of a modem is measured in bits per second (bps).
  • High bandwidth provides less data traffic, whereas low bandwidth provides high data traffic.
1.1 · Bandwidth and Throughput

Bandwidth of internet services

The term bits per second (bps) is used to refer to the speed of different types of Internet services. Faster services have higher bandwidth than slower ones.

ServiceBandwidth
Dial-upLow bandwidth
DSLAs slow as 128 Kbps. Many providers offer 768 Kbps and over.
CableStarts at 1 Mbps and can theoretically go as high as 30 Mbps.
SatelliteBetween 1 Mbps and 3 Mbps.

Narrow bandwidth provides high data traffic, whereas wide bandwidth provides low data traffic.

1.1 · Bandwidth and Throughput

Throughput

DefinitionThroughput is the actual amount of data that is successfully sent or received over a network in a given time. It shows how much of your available bandwidth is being used.

Traffic or roadblocks reduce how many cars pass. That 7 cars per minute is your throughput.

1.1 · Bandwidth and Throughput

Bandwidth and throughput compared

  • Throughput is measured in Mbps, like bandwidth.
  • Throughput is usually less than bandwidth because of delays, interference or network issues.
  • Higher throughput means faster and smoother internet performance.

Bandwidth

The maximum possible speed.

Throughput

The real speed you are getting.

1.1 · Mobile Network Generations (3G, 4G and 5G)

Mobile network generations

Mobile networks are used to make calls, transmit messages and access the internet from mobile phones. As technology advances, new generations of mobile networks are introduced to meet growing communication demands. Every generation improves speed, quality and services.

3G

Improved mobile internet.

4G

Made it quicker and smoother.

5G

Will support new technologies in the future.

1.1 · Mobile Network Generations

3G (Third Generation)

3G represents the third generation of mobile network technology. It increased the use and popularity of mobile internet.

  • Provides internet access for mobile phones.
  • Supports video calling and video streaming.
  • Faster than the 2G network.
  • Internet speed is moderate.
  • Launched in the early 2000s.
  • Used for browsing websites, emails and social media.
1.1 · Mobile Network Generations

4G (Fourth Generation)

4G represents the fourth generation of mobile network technology. It is significantly quicker than 3G.

  • Provides high-speed internet.
  • Supports HD video streaming and online gaming.
  • Better quality voice and video calls.
  • Faster download and upload speeds.
  • Launched around 2010.
  • Useful for apps like YouTube, Zoom and online classes.
1.1 · Mobile Network Generations

5G (Fifth Generation)

5G is the most recent iteration of mobile network technology. It is intended for high speed and low latency.

  • Very high internet speed.
  • Very low delay (latency).
  • Supports smart devices, IoT and smart cities.
  • Useful for virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR).
  • Launched around 2020.
  • Can connect many devices at the same time.

1.1 · Data Packets

Data packets

DefinitionData packets are small data units used to transmit information across a network, such as the Internet.

When a large file (such as a movie, image or message) is transmitted, it is first divided into numerous small packets. The packets are sent via the network individually and reassembled at the destination.

  • Imagine sending a letter in parts. You write a big letter and cut it into 5 pieces (packets).
  • You send each piece in its envelope.
  • The receiver puts the pieces together to read the full letter.
  • That is how the internet sends videos, files and web pages.
1.1 · Data Packets

What a packet contains

  • Improve data transmission speed and efficiency.
  • Can take multiple paths to reach the destination.
  • Reduce network congestion.
  • Enable error detection and resending of lost packets.
1.1 · Frequency

Frequency

DefinitionFrequency means how many times something happens in one second. In networks and mobile communication, it describes how often radio waves or signals repeat every second.

It is used in Wi-Fi, mobile networks and radio communication. The frequency is like the heartbeat of a signal, which is how fast its "beats" every second.

UnitMeaning
1 Hz1 wave per second
1 MHz1 million waves per second
1 GHz1 billion waves per second

Frequency is measured in Hertz (Hz).

1.1 · Frequency

High and low frequency

Higher frequencies

Faster speed, but shorter range.

Lower frequencies

Slower speed, but longer range.

Different mobile generations use different frequency bands. Example: 4G may use 800 MHz or 1800 MHz. 5G can use very high frequencies like 28 GHz for super-fast internet.
