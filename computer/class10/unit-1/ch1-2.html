1.2 · Communication Media

Communication media (transmission media)

DefinitionThe wired or wireless means through which data travels from one point to another, or from sender to receiver, is called communication media.

  • It is also called transmission media or communication channel.
  • Data travels in the form of electronic current, radio and microwave. The media act as a channel linking computing devices so that they can interact with each other.
  • The most commonly used media include cable, satellite, microwaves and fiber-optics.
1.2 · Communication Media

Signals and communication devices

Computers and telecommunication devices use signals to represent data. These signals are transmitted from one device to another in the form of electromagnetic signals.

Communication devices are the tools used by senders and receivers to send and receive messages.

Common examples of communication devices

TelephonesCellular phonesWireless mobile devicesGPS technology systemsRadiosTransmitters
1.2 · Communication Media

Types of communication media

There are two types of communication media.

1.2 · Wired Media

Wired or guided media

DefinitionWired media use different types of wires, such as twisted-pair cable, coaxial cable and fiber-optic cable, to transfer data among computers and other devices.

  • It is also known as guided or bound media. Data are transmitted within a closed path using interconnected wire or cable.
  • It refers to all types of physical wire through which data travels from one location to another.
  • Wired communication channels are specially used to connect computers and devices locally.
1.2 · Wired Media

Twisted-pair cable

The twisted-pair cable is the most commonly used transmission media. It is widely used in telephone lines, so it is often called a telephone wire. It consists of copper wires twisted into pairs, with each wire insulated with plastic.

UTP

Unshielded Twisted Pair. Cables without a shield.

STP

Shielded Twisted Pair. Cables with a shield.

  • Characteristics: low cost, easy to install, high speed capacity, high attenuation, 100 meter limit.
  • Its main drawback is short distance due to attenuation.
1.2 · Wired Media

CAT6

CAT6 stands for Category 6 cable. It is a type of network cable used to connect computers and devices to the internet or a local network (LAN) using wires. It is faster and higher in quality than older cables, such as CAT5.

It helps transfer data fast and smoothly between devices like

ComputersRoutersSwitchesInternet modems
  • Used for computer networks and internet connectivity.
  • Achieves high data transfer speeds of up to 10 Gbps over short distances.
  • Performs well at a frequency of 250 MHz, higher than CAT5e.
  • Reduced interference and noise (crosstalk).
  • Ideal for LAN connections. Widely used in schools, offices and households. Supports Ethernet connections.
1.2 · Wired Media

Fiber-optic cable

Fiber-optic is a technology that uses flexible glass or plastic threads (fibers) to transmit data. A thin filament of glass fiber is wrapped in a protective jacket, and multiple fibers are placed in the same cable.

  • Data is transmitted in the form of light pulses at very high speed, close to the speed of light.
  • The data transmission rate is billions of bits per second. It is more powerful than coaxial and twisted-pair cable, and is not affected by electromagnetic radiation.
  • The data error is very low. It is the most widely used cable today.
  • Main weakness: it is fragile and difficult to install.
1.2 · Wired Media

Features of fiber-optic cable

  • It is a glass or plastic-based communication cable.
  • It sends data using light pulses rather than electrical signals.
  • Provides high-speed data transmission.
  • You can transfer data over vast distances without losing signal.
  • It has a high bandwidth, allowing it to transfer a large amount of data.
  • Not affected by electromagnetic interference.
  • Ensures clear and secure communication.
  • Lightweight and thinner than copper cables.
  • Used for internet, phone, cable TV and networking.
  • It supports current technologies such as 5G networks and broadband internet.
1.2 · Wireless Media

Wireless or unguided media

Wireless technology is a rapidly growing, modern communication technology. Due to its flexibility, it is becoming more popular.

  • It uses radio signals or infrared signals for transmitting and receiving data.
  • It is also known as unguided or unbound media, because the signals are not bounded to a particular direction as in a wired system.
  • Unguided channels are used to connect devices where cabling is not possible.

The commonly used wireless communication systems

Wi-FiBluetoothRFIDCommunication satellite
1.2 · Wi-Fi Technology

Wi-Fi (Wireless Fidelity)

DefinitionWi-Fi is a wireless technology that allows devices to access the Internet through radio signals instead of physical cables.

  • We connect phones, laptops, tablets and smart TVs to the Internet without wires because of Wi-Fi.
  • It mainly operates on the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz frequency bands.
  • Devices connect through a Wi-Fi access point called a router, which receives Internet service from the provider and transmits it wirelessly to nearby devices.
  • The technology is based on standards developed by the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE).
1.2 · Wi-Fi Technology

How Wi-Fi sends information

DSSS and OFDM

Direct-Sequence Spread Spectrum (DSSS) and Orthogonal Frequency-Division Multiplexing (OFDM) help reduce interference and improve signal quality.

Channel bonding

Combines two smaller channels, such as two 20 MHz channels, into a larger 40 MHz channel to increase speed.

Beamforming

Directs the wireless signal toward a specific device instead of spreading it equally in all directions, making the connection stronger and more stable.

Wi-Fi allows multiple devices to connect simultaneously, making it a fast, reliable and convenient wireless alternative to wired networks in homes, schools, offices and public places.

1.2 · Wi-Fi Technology

Features of Wi-Fi

  • Provides wireless connectivity to the devices to be connected to the internet.
  • Works at short and medium distances.
  • Offers high-speed internet connectivity.
  • Commonly used in homes, schools, offices and public areas.
  • Supports several devices simultaneously.
  • Data transfer is carried out by radio waves.
1.2 · Bluetooth Technology

Bluetooth technology

DefinitionBluetooth is a wireless communication technology that allows devices to exchange data over short distances without using cables.

  • Unlike Wi-Fi, which is mainly used for internet access, Bluetooth is designed for direct device-to-device connections.
  • It operates on the 2.4 GHz frequency band and can connect devices within a range of about 10 meters. Some modern versions can reach up to 100 meters.

Devices that use Bluetooth

SmartphonesHeadphonesSpeakersKeyboardsMiceSmartwatches
1.2 · Bluetooth Technology

How Bluetooth works

  • Bluetooth uses frequency hopping spread spectrum (FHSS). It rapidly switches frequencies within the 2.4 GHz band, which reduces interference from other wireless devices and improves connection reliability.
  • Modern versions, such as Bluetooth 5.0, offer faster data transfer rates, longer range and lower power consumption.
  • Bluetooth allows multiple devices to connect in a small network called a piconet, where one device acts as the master and the others act as slaves.
  • Because of its low power requirements and short range, Bluetooth is widely used for personal area networks (PANs) and connecting peripheral devices.
1.2 · Bluetooth Technology

Features of Bluetooth

  • Bluetooth is a short-range wireless communication technology.
  • It is used to wirelessly connect devices like headphones, keyboards, speakers, computers and other Bluetooth-compatible devices.
  • It works over a short distance (about 10 meters for most standard devices).
  • It consumes little power, helping to extend battery life.
  • It is simple to use and does not require an internet connection.
  • It communicates using radio waves.
1.2 · RFID

Radio Frequency Identification (RFID)

DefinitionRFID is a wireless technology used to identify and track objects using radio waves.

An RFID system has two main parts: an RFID tag and an RFID reader. The tag stores information about the object, which the reader can detect without needing a direct line of sight.

1.2 · RFID

RFID tags and uses

Passive tags

Do not have a battery and rely on the reader's signal.

Active tags

Have a battery and can communicate over longer distances.

  • RFID operates on different frequency bands depending on the application.
  • Supermarkets: automatic billing. Libraries: track books. Transport: toll collection. Offices and schools: access control. Warehouses: manage inventory.
  • Example: in a library, when you bring a book near the RFID scanner, it automatically detects which book it is, eliminating the need for barcode scanning.
1.2 · RFID

Common uses and features of RFID

Common uses

ID cards in schools and offices. Inventory management in shops. Animal tracking (tags on pets or livestock). Public transport cards (like tapping a card to get on a bus).

Features

Wireless technology for identifying and tracking items. Transfers data via radio waves. Does not require physical interaction. Operates automatically and quickly. Includes an RFID tag and an RFID reader.

1.2 · Communication Satellite

Communication satellite

DefinitionA communication satellite is an artificial satellite placed in orbit around the Earth. Its main job is to relay and transmit signals over long distances.

It receives signals from a ground station, amplifies them and sends them to another station. This allows people to communicate across countries and continents using television, telephone, radio and the Internet.

1.2 · Communication Satellite

Orbits and uses of satellites

Geostationary

Stay above the same point on Earth.

Low Earth orbit

Move closer to the Earth and cover smaller areas.

Molniya / elliptical

Used for special coverage.

  • Placing and maintaining a satellite is expensive and requires careful planning. Satellite communication has been in use since 1965.
  • Widely used for TV broadcasting, weather forecasting, GPS navigation, military communication, internet connectivity and telephone networks.
  • Satellites allow signals to reach areas that are difficult to cover with cables.
1.2 · Communication Satellite

Features of satellite communication

  • Sends and receives signals using artificial satellites in space.
  • It covers huge areas, including distant locations.
  • Used for TV broadcasting, GPS, weather forecasting and internet access.
  • Facilitates long-distance communication.
  • Useful during natural catastrophes when other networks fail.
  • Signals are transmitted by microwaves.
