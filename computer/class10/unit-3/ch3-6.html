3.6 · Concept

Concept of animation

DefinitionAnimation is the process of creating an illusion of movement by rapidly displaying a sequence of static images that differ minimally from each other.

  • The human brain has persistence of vision: an image lingers in the retina for a fraction of a second after it is gone.
  • When a series of images (frames) is shown quickly, the brain blends them and perceives smooth, continuous motion.
3.6 · Purpose

Purpose and applications of animation

Entertainment

Films, TV series and web shorts.

Education

Explains complex concepts simply.

Advertising

Memorable, persuasive commercials.

Video games

Brings game worlds and characters to life.

3.6 · Purpose

More applications of animation

Simulations

Training pilots, surgeons and military personnel in a risk-free environment.

Scientific visualization

Modeling things too small, fast or large to see, such as molecules and galaxies.

User interface

Subtle animations improve apps and websites.

3.6 · 2D

2D animation

Definition2D animation takes place in a two-dimensional space. It measures height and width but not depth. Images are drawn on a flat surface and shown one after another.

  • Traditional 2D animation hand-drew each frame on paper or transparent sheets called cels.
  • Today it is made with software such as Adobe Animate, Toon Boom Harmony, Synfig Studio, OpenToonz, Krita and Pencil2D.
  • Techniques: frame-by-frame animation or tweening. Used in cartoons, commercials, educational videos and games. Example: Tom and Jerry.
3.6 · 3D

3D animation

Definition3D animation creates moving images in a three-dimensional space, using height, width and depth. Characters and objects look more realistic and alive.

  • Uses computer tools such as Autodesk Maya, Blender or 3Ds Max to generate digital models that can be turned and viewed from any angle.
  • Examples: Toy Story, Frozen and Avatar.
3.6 · 3D

How 3D animation is made

1. Model

Build 3D models of characters and settings.

2. Rig

Add a digital skeleton so models can move.

3. Animate

Move the models frame by frame.

4. Texture and light

Add textures, colors and lighting for realism.

5. Render

All frames are rendered to create the finished video.

3.6 · Difference

2D vs 3D animation

2D animation3D animation
Two dimensions: height and widthThree dimensions: height, width and depth
Flat, cartoon-like appearanceRealistic, with depth
Drawing images on a flat surfaceBuilding digital 3D models on the computer
Adobe Animate, Toon Boom Harmony, Synfig StudioBlender, Autodesk Maya, 3ds Max

2D vs 3D animation (continued)

2D animation3D animation

Easier and less expensiveMore complex, takes more time and computer power
Cartoons, educational videos, simple gamesMovies, video games, architecture, virtual reality
Tom and Jerry, The SimpsonsToy Story, Frozen, Avatar
  • Choose 2D for simple, artistic or budget-friendly animation. Choose 3D for realism, depth and modern effects.
3.6 · Summary

Key terms to remember

Animation

Illusion of movement from still images.

Persistence of vision

Image lingers briefly in the eye.

2D

Height and width. Flat. Cheaper.

3D

Height, width and depth. Realistic.

Rigging

Adding a skeleton to a 3D model.
